# Product study: Blinq

Research date: 24 July 2026

## What Blinq is

Blinq has expanded beyond digital business cards into a broad contact and relationship-capture platform. Its current product has four connected jobs:

1. **Share identity** through a web card, QR code, link, NFC card, Apple/Google Wallet, smartwatch, widgets, email signatures, and virtual meeting backgrounds.
2. **Capture people** by scanning business cards, badges, QR codes, and LinkedIn profiles or by requesting reciprocal contact details.
3. **Remember context** with an AI notetaker that associates conversation notes with a contact.
4. **Activate data** through CRM sync, enrichment, tagging, campaigns, attribution, team analytics, provisioning, and admin controls.

Its public positioning is “Share who you are and remember who you meet.” Blinq says it serves more than four million users across 500,000 companies.

## Primary customer groups

### Individuals

Professionals who want a polished, always-current identity card without printing physical cards.

### Small and medium teams

Teams that need centrally controlled branding, staff cards, signatures, contact capture, event campaigns, and CRM sync.

### Enterprises

Organizations that need SSO, SCIM, provisioning, security compliance, subteam administration, custom reporting, contracts, and support.

### Event and field-sales teams

Exhibitors and sellers who need badge/card scanning, lead qualification, conversation capture, enrichment, attribution, and immediate CRM handoff.

## Core product mechanics

### Acquisition loop

A user shares a card with a non-user. The recipient sees the web card and can save the contact or share their own details. Every share is therefore a potential product-discovery event.

### Retention loop

The user updates one profile and distributes it through several surfaces. Contact capture, notes, and CRM sync create a stored relationship history that increases switching cost.

### Expansion loop

One professional starts individually, demonstrates the product during meetings, and can later introduce team-level branding and administration to their employer.

## Current pricing snapshot

- Free: $0, with up to two cards, unlimited sharing and contact creation, Wallet support, a personal email signature, and virtual backgrounds.
- Premium: $9.99 monthly or $7.33/month billed annually, with up to five cards, universal scanning, AI notes, enrichment, branding, and exports.
- Business: $6.99 monthly or $4.99/month billed annually per card, with a five-card minimum. It adds team management, provisioning, templates, lead capture, campaigns, CRM integrations, and analytics.
- Enterprise: custom pricing for organizations with more complex security, provisioning, reporting, support, and commercial needs.

Prices and packaging can change and should be rechecked before using them commercially.

## Why the product is strong

- The recipient does not need an app.
- Sharing works across many physical and digital contexts.
- The free card creates a natural viral loop.
- A profile can stay current after it has been shared.
- Blinq connects the moment of meeting to systems of record.
- It serves individuals, teams, event sellers, and enterprises with one underlying contact graph.
- The combination of identity, capture, notes, enrichment, and workflow is more defensible than a QR-card generator alone.

## Where a new product can differentiate

Competing feature-for-feature would be expensive and strategically weak. Better opportunities are:

### A sharper target user

Blinq is horizontal. A vertical product can offer vocabulary, fields, workflows, templates, and integrations designed for one profession.

### Follow-up as the hero

Most digital-card products celebrate the exchange. The unmet job begins afterward: remembering why the person mattered and taking the right next action.

### Personal relationship intelligence

A user may want a private relationship workspace rather than a traditional sales CRM: last interaction, promises made, shared interests, reminders, warm-introduction paths, and relationship health.

### Simpler team economics

A small-team product could use workspace pricing instead of charging per card and separately for captured leads.

### Privacy-first AI

Conversation recording creates consent, trust, accuracy, and data-retention concerns. A product can begin with user-dictated post-meeting notes and transparent extraction rather than ambient recording.

## Risks and lessons

- A digital card alone is commoditized and easy to reproduce.
- Native OS surfaces and NFC hardware add operational complexity but are not required for validation.
- Contact enrichment can be costly and carries privacy/compliance obligations.
- CRM integrations are deceptively deep: field mapping, deduplication, ownership, conflict resolution, and authentication matter.
- Recording in-person conversations raises consent-law and user-trust issues across jurisdictions.
- Enterprise security claims require real controls and audits, not marketing language.
- Network effects are limited if recipients never need an account; durable value must also come from stored workflow and relationship history.
